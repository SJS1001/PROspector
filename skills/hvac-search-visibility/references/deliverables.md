# Contractor program deliverables

Read this reference when creating or substantially overhauling an HVAC search,
content, measurement, or website program.

Produce only the artifacts the user needs. A comprehensive reusable package can
contain the following.

## Resource overview

Lead with:

- audience, geography, evidence snapshot, and explicit exclusions;
- what the package is and is not;
- priority order for a contractor;
- evidence labels and source index;
- links to the playbook, UX contract, validation ledger, examples, and research.

Say plainly whether the result is documentation, coded UI, or deployed software.

## Staged playbook

### Stage 0 — Truth and authority

Create the contractor truth register, service/location table, credential record,
risk register, approval roles, and stop conditions.

### Stage 1 — Data governance and measurement

Complete the blocking data-governance gate before enabling measurement. Define
events according to actual transitions:

- call click versus connected/qualified call;
- lead generated versus appointment requested;
- appointment requested versus confirmed;
- qualified/disqualified lead with controlled reasons;
- closed sale/unconverted lead with system-of-record evidence.

Keep business outcomes above diagnostic metrics. Search impressions, rankings,
AI citations, and crawler visits are not booked jobs.

### Stage 2 — Local entity and profile integrity

Require an authorized actor, prior-state export, exact change log, small coherent
change sets, last-owner protection, phone/URL safeguards, monitoring, and a
re-verification/suspension/access-recovery plan. Never change a live profile
solely from prose or a fixture.

### Stage 3 — Website and conversion foundation

Specify canonical pages, crawl/index controls, mobile parity, visible/schema
parity, service-area confirmation, truthful trust evidence, emergency routing,
privacy, accessible forms, failure recovery, and request-versus-confirmation
states.

### Stage 4 — Service and local content

Prioritize real service pages and user decisions before generic articles.
Require a brief with:

- homeowner job-to-be-done and direct answer;
- real service and geography;
- symptoms, options, exclusions, process, price factors, and next step;
- contractor-specific proof and consent;
- claim/safety sources, assumptions, author/reviewer, and review date;
- internal links, schema decision, measurement, and rollback.

Treat publication order as a contractor hypothesis informed by demand, capacity,
margin, seasonality, and risk—not a platform ranking rule.

### Stage 5 — Reviews and off-page evidence

Use a neutral completed-interaction rule, stable request key, cross-channel
deduplication, cooldown/frequency cap, opt-out/suppression, human-reviewed
responses, and an audit trail. Pursue only accurate, relevant directories,
associations, utilities, programs, and community references.

### Stage 6 — AI evaluation

Begin only after ordinary search, content, and measurement foundations. Use
repeated versioned samples, record retrieval conditions and variability, and
compare diagnostic visibility with qualified outcomes. Bound changes and protect
traditional search/lead performance from harm.

## UX contract

At minimum, define:

- global mobile shell and call/request actions;
- emergency strip separated from sales;
- trust and credential presentation;
- home, service, and location page modules;
- request-service fields and all states;
- privacy/contact controls;
- content presentation;
- accessibility and performance acceptance;
- prohibited dark patterns.

Request-service states should include initial, service-area mismatch, validation
error, submitting, network/server error, received, and confirmed appointment.
Never label a request as booked before operational acceptance.

## Measurement hierarchy

Use a hierarchy such as:

1. booked/sold work, revenue, gross profit, cancellations, capacity;
2. qualified leads, service/geography match, appointment rate;
3. connected calls, completed forms, confirmed bookings;
4. Business Profile interactions;
5. Search Console performance;
6. AI referrals/citations and repeated prompt observations;
7. indexing, errors, Core Web Vitals, uptime.

Annotate weather, promotions, media spend, price, staffing, capacity, outages,
tracking changes, and platform changes. Do not claim causality from a before/after
chart alone.

## 30/60/90 framing

Use time boxes as a planning hypothesis, not a completion guarantee.

- Days 1–30: truth, authority, risk, data governance, baseline, profile plan.
- Days 31–60: bounded profile corrections, website/UX foundation, core services.
- Days 61–90: reviews/off-page controls, local proof, controlled AI evaluation.

Each phase remains incomplete until its evidence and live acceptance gates pass.
