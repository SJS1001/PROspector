# ONE for Mining — Sales & Marketing Plan

Built from the locked ICP (see ICP-MINING.md) and the ONE materials, applying frameworks from coreyhaines31/marketingskills: product-marketing (positioning), cold-email (outbound), sales-enablement (phone/objections), social (content). Plan-only: no outreach tools assumed connected. Voice throughout matches the deck — understated, evidence-led, no hype, AI mentioned late.

## 1. Positioning foundation (product-marketing)

**One-liner:** ONE is the decision layer for a mine's uptime, recovery and throughput — it turns trapped equipment, process, lab and maintenance data into decisions that protect production.

**Category:** operations intelligence / decision layer for mineral processing (sits *above* historians and dashboards; not a historian, not a SCADA, not a BI tool).

**What it does:** ingests your existing sources, harmonizes them into one operating model, surfaces the recommendation with evidence, routes it to the right person as an alert/task, and learns. Connects read-only over a segmented OT/IT link; client keeps data and the models trained on it.

**Business model:** fixed-scope diagnostic fee → annual platform subscription + integration services scoped to the site.

**Proof posture (from the grill):** "Live" = the platform runs in production on real data at Engebø (Nordic Mining), a named, permissioned reference. Engebø figures are masked for client data security — real, not unproven. Proof a prospect can verify = (1) reference call with Nordic Mining under NDA, (2) the 8-week diagnostic on their own data.

**Differentiators:** (1) decision layer that acts on data, not just stores/shows it; (2) unifies OEM/vendor silos a historian leaves fragmented; (3) read-only, client-owned data + models — low security friction; (4) live production reference; (5) sequenced trust model — core analytics now, agents later, nothing acts on its own until the core is hardened; (6) **team pedigree** — see below.

**Why us / team pedigree (Rolls-Royce aerospace & maritime):** Members of the team built decision, condition-monitoring and digital-twin systems at Rolls-Royce in aerospace and maritime — domains where an unplanned failure grounds a fleet or strands a ship, not just costs a shift. Aerospace pioneered per-asset digital twins and outcome-based "power-by-the-hour" uptime (Rolls-Royce has run condition-based maintenance this way for 20+ years, lifting time-on-wing and time-between-maintenance materially). ONE brings that reliability-engineering discipline to mineral processing. This pedigree (a) answers the "can a young company actually do this?" question, (b) makes the digital-twin roadmap credible, and (c) separates ONE from data-plumbing rivals who lack heavy-asset reliability heritage.
**Guardrail:** use strictly as team *background*. Never state or imply Rolls-Royce is a customer, partner, investor, or endorser. Phrase as "team with backgrounds at Rolls-Royce in aerospace and maritime." No RR logos/marks.

**Words to use:** decision layer, operating model, uptime/recovery/throughput, read-only, your data stays yours, baseline, ramp-up. **Avoid:** autonomous mining, "drives the work," "AI-powered" up front, decision latency (internal term), unaudited.

## 3. Outbound email (cold-email skill)

Principles applied: peer voice not vendor, you/your dominates, personalization tied to the trigger, one low-friction ask, boring subject lines, each follow-up adds a new angle. Personalize the bracketed trigger from the nightly digest's matched pain #.

### Sequence A — Operating / ramp-up plant (champion: Plant/Met Manager)

**Touch 1 — subject: `ramp-up`**
> [Saw {Mine} commissioned the new {circuit} this year.] Ramps usually stall in the same place: the signal to fix recovery exists in the data, it just arrives a shift too late to act on.
>
> We run an eight-week diagnostic on your own plant data and hand back a measured baseline of what that lag is costing — no rip-and-replace, read-only, your data stays yours.
>
> Worth a look at whether the same gap is on your circuit?

**Touch 2 (+4 days) — subject: `your data`**
> One thing that makes the diagnostic easy to say yes to: it's read-only over a segmented OT/IT link, and the models we build train on your data and stay with you. Nothing leaves the fence.
>
> Happy to send a one-page security note your controls team can sanity-check. Useful?

**Touch 3 (+5 days) — subject: `Engebø`**
> We're live in production at Engebø with Nordic Mining on real plant data. I can't share their numbers, but they'll take a reference call under NDA.
>
> If hitting nameplate faster is on your plate this year, that call is the fastest way to judge whether this is real. Want the intro?

**Touch 4 (+7 days, breakup) — subject: `closing the loop`**
> I'll stop here so I'm not cluttering your inbox. If recovery drift or unplanned downtime becomes the priority, the eight-week diagnostic turns the guesswork into a number on your own data. The door's open.

### Sequence B — Greenfield (Project Director / Commissioning Manager)

**Touch 1 — subject: `before first ore`**
> [Congrats on reaching {FID/financing} at {Project}.] The mines that hit nameplate fastest tend to design the decision layer in during construction — not bolt it on after eating the ramp-up losses.
>
> We do a fixed-scope commissioning-readiness review that gets your equipment, process and lab data into one operating model so it's live on day one. Worth a conversation while the plant's still on the drawing board?

**Touch 2 (+5 days) — subject: `day one`**
> Quick proof it's real: ONE is in production at Engebø with Nordic Mining today. The readiness review now converts into a measured diagnostic at first ore — so you start operating from a baseline, not a blank slate. Useful to explore?

**Touch 3 (+7 days, breakup) — subject: `timing`**
> Commissioning windows close fast, so I'll leave it here. When you're scoping operational readiness, designing the decision layer in early is far cheaper than retrofitting. Happy to pick it up then.

## 4. Phone / call plan (sales-enablement)

**Cold-call opener (champion, ~20 sec):**
> "Hi {name} — I'll be quick. We help processing plants get recovery and uptime decisions out of the data they already have, before the shift report makes it history. You're [ramping the new circuit / I saw the recovery note] — is hitting nameplate something you're actively working on right now?"

**Discovery questions (map to pains):**
1. Where are you against nameplate today, and what's holding the last bit back? *(#3)*
2. When recovery drifts, how long before someone acts on it? *(#2, #4)*
3. How many systems does an operator check to make one call? *(#6)*
4. Last unplanned outage — did anything warn you? *(#5)*
5. Can you actually get data out of your OEM kit, or is it locked in their portal? *(#1)*
6. Who'd need to bless a read-only OT/IT connection? *(maps buying committee)*

**Objection-handling quick table:**

| Objection | Real concern | Response + proof | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|
| "We already have PI/Grafana." | Sunk cost, "why more software" | "Those store and show — ONE decides on top of them. We sit above your historian, we don't replace it." | "How long today between a signal and someone acting on it?" |
| "Security won't allow plant data out." | OT risk | "Read-only over a segmented OT/IT link; data and models stay inside your fence. I'll send the security one-pager." | "Can I get your controls/OT lead on the next call?" |
| "Is this proven or a demo?" | Vendor risk | "Live in production at Engebø with Nordic Mining. Figures are masked for their security, but they'll do an NDA reference call." | "Want the intro?" |
| "No budget this quarter." | Timing/ROI | "That's what the fixed-scope diagnostic is for — small, time-boxed, and it produces the number you'd take to budget." | "What would the number need to show to make it a yes?" |
| "Send me information." | Brush-off | "I'll send a one-pager — but the fastest read is 20 minutes on your circuit. Worth it?" | Book the slot. |
| "You're young/small — can you deliver?" | Vendor risk | "Our team built condition-monitoring and digital-twin systems at Rolls-Royce in aerospace and maritime — where a failure grounds a fleet. We brought that discipline to mineral processing, and it's live at Engebø today." | "Want to see how that reliability approach maps to your circuit?" |

**Sample 1-week LinkedIn calendar:**

| Day | Post |

**Three sample posts (hooks from the social skill):**

> **(Contrarian hook)** "Unpopular opinion: your plant doesn't need more sensors.
> The signal to fix recovery already exists in your data. It just arrives a shift too late to act on. The constraint isn't sensing — it's access and the lag before a decision gets made. Fix the lag and the tonnes show up. We measure that lag on a plant's own data in eight weeks."

> **(Value hook)** "How to find out what ramp-up lag is costing you (without ripping anything out):
> 1. Pick one decision you make late today. 2. Measure how long from signal to action. 3. Multiply by the throughput you lose in that window. That number is usually bigger than anyone expects — and it's the whole reason the decision layer exists."

> **(Story/pedigree hook)** "In aerospace, you don't get to 'find out next morning' that an engine is failing.
> Our team built condition-monitoring and digital-twin systems at Rolls-Royce — aerospace and maritime, where an unplanned failure grounds a fleet or strands a ship. The discipline is the same one mineral processing needs: see the problem in the data before it costs you. That's what we brought to mining." *(team background only — not an RR endorsement)*

> **(Story/proof hook)** "A mine in Norway runs its operating model on live plant data today.
> I can't show you their numbers — that's their data, and keeping it theirs is the point. But it's in production, and read-only: nothing leaves the fence. That's the bar we think 'live' should mean. Most 'AI for mining' demos can't clear it."

**Engagement routine (30 min/day):** reply to all comments; comment with real insight on 5–10 posts from target plants/owners/met-engineering voices; reshare relevant industry news with a take. Identify 30–50 target accounts/people and engage consistently.

## 7. 90-day rollout & KPIs

**Days 1–30 — foundation:** finalize positioning + one-pagers + persona cards; stand up LinkedIn cadence (company + Steven's personal); nightly prospector feeds the list; build the two cold-email sequences into whatever sender you adopt.
**Days 31–60 — outbound on:** work the nightly digest daily — both motions per account; book reference calls; start the greenfield readiness conversations; weekly social review, double down on what lands.
**Days 61–90 — convert:** push qualified accounts to a diagnostic SOW; line up the first non-Engebø diagnostic as your second reference; codify a lightweight playbook from what's working.

**KPIs:** leads surfaced/night (quality > volume); reply rate (target peer-grade, ~5–10%+ on well-triggered touches); reference calls booked; diagnostics scoped; LinkedIn: engagement rate + inbound DMs from target roles. Track the diagnostic as the single conversion action everything points to.
