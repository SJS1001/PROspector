# ONE for Mining — Competitor Battlecard (verified)

Every "mining footprint" claim below was web-verified (June 2026); sources at the end. Where a claim could not be verified, it is marked **[unverified]** — do not assert it to a prospect until confirmed. Use ONE's differentiators honestly: ONE is a *decision layer that sits above* historians/automation, read-only, client-owned, with aerospace/maritime reliability pedigree — not a historian, MES, or APC replacement.

## How to frame the category (say this first)
"Most of these are either where your data is *stored* (historians), where your equipment is *controlled* (OEM/APC), or a big horizontal data platform you staff up to build on. ONE is the thin decision layer that sits on top of what you already run, read-only, and turns it into one operating model — live in weeks, not a multi-year platform build."

---

## 1. AVEVA / PI System (formerly OSIsoft) — the incumbent historian
**Verified mining footprint:** YES. AVEVA markets explicitly to mining/metals/minerals; named users incl. Vale, Norsk Hydro, ArcelorMittal, Hindalco; 70,000+ point PI deployment at a major copper operation (Cobre Panama).
**What it is:** the dominant real-time data historian + data infrastructure. Stores and displays time-series plant data.
**Overlap with ONE:** data ingestion / single source of time-series truth.
**How ONE wins:** Don't fight it — **sit on top of it.** "PI stores and shows; it doesn't decide. ONE reads from PI and closes the loop into a recommendation and a task. We make your PI investment do more, read-only." Most defensible posture: PI is a *connector*, not a competitor.
**Watch-out:** if the buyer thinks "we already have PI dashboards, that's enough," lead with pain #2 (decision lag) and #6 (disconnected dashboards) — exactly what a historian leaves unsolved.

## 2. Palantir Foundry — the horizontal AI/ops platform
**Verified mining footprint:** YES. Rio Tinto is a multi-year Foundry customer (signed 2021, renewed/extended 4 yrs Nov 2024); uses Foundry's "Ontology" as a digital twin for plant ops, geotechnical risk, and autonomous trains.
**What it is:** a powerful, general-purpose data-integration + operational-decision platform. Heavy, broad, enterprise-scale.
**Overlap with ONE:** "operating model / decision layer" language overlaps most here.
**How ONE wins:** **speed, focus, and cost.** "Foundry is a platform you staff a team to build on over quarters. ONE is a purpose-built mineral-processing decision layer that's live in an 8-week diagnostic on one decision. You get a measured baseline before a Foundry program would finish scoping." Also: ONE is read-only and domain-shaped out of the box; Foundry is horizontal and integration-heavy.
**Watch-out:** at super-majors (Rio Tinto scale) Foundry may already be embedded — those are *multiplier/enterprise* accounts, not your beachhead. Aim ONE at single/few-site operators where a Foundry program is overkill.

## 3. AspenTech — Aspen Mtell (prescriptive/predictive maintenance)
**Verified mining footprint:** YES. Dedicated metals & mining offering; Aspen Mtell case studies incl. a Canadian metals & mining company and a zinc/lead smelter (predicted a critical pump failure 40 days out); markets to haul fleets, crushers, processing plants.
**What it is:** AI-driven asset-health / failure prediction (maintenance-centric).
**Overlap with ONE:** pain #5 (downtime / reactive maintenance).
**How ONE wins:** **scope.** "Mtell tells you a machine is about to fail. ONE covers the whole operating decision — recovery, throughput and grade, not just asset health — and unifies process + lab + maintenance into one view, not a maintenance silo." Position Mtell as a point solution; ONE as the operating model.
**Watch-out:** if the buyer's only pain is maintenance, this is a real, credible incumbent — pivot the conversation to recovery/throughput (#3, #4) where ONE is broader.

## 4. OEM digital arms — Metso (verified), FLSmidth, Weir
**Verified mining footprint:** Metso = YES (Metso Metrics: condition monitoring + predictive maintenance for comminution; plus process optimization / advanced process control for mining). FLSmidth (ECS/ProcessExpert) and Weir (Synertrex) **[unverified — confirm specifics before asserting]**.
**What it is:** equipment makers bundling monitoring/optimization for *their own* kit.
**Overlap with ONE:** condition monitoring + process optimization on specific equipment.
**How ONE wins:** **vendor-neutral unification.** "OEM platforms optimize their own machines and lock data in their portal — that's literally pain #1. ONE is vendor-neutral: it reads across every OEM, lab and historian into one operating model you own." This is your sharpest line against OEM lock-in.
**Watch-out:** the OEM is often already on-site with a relationship. Frame ONE as the layer *above* all OEMs, not a rip-and-replace.

## 5. Cognite — adjacent industrial DataOps (NOT a verified mining specialist)
**Verified mining footprint:** NO (as of June 2026). Cognite Data Fusion is a Nordic (Aker-backed) industrial DataOps platform; verified customers are oil & gas / heavy industry (Aker BP, OMV, Cosmo Oil; SLB partnership). No mining customers surfaced in verification.
**What it is:** horizontal contextualized-industrial-data platform, oil-&-gas-led.
**How ONE wins / how to talk about it:** treat as an **adjacent threat, not a current mining rival.** "Cognite is a strong DataOps platform from energy — but it's a horizontal data layer you build on, not a mineral-processing decision layer. And like Foundry, it's a program, not an 8-week diagnostic." Because Cognite is Nordic/Aker-affiliated (your backyard), watch for it entering mining.
**Do NOT say:** "Cognite is big in mining" — unverified and likely wrong; it dents your credibility.

## Where ONE is uniquely strong (use against all of them)
- **Decision layer, not storage/control/platform** — sits above the stack you already run, read-only.
- **Vendor-neutral** — unifies OEM/historian/lab silos (the incumbents each own a slice).
- **Time-to-value** — 8-week diagnostic on one decision, on the client's own data, vs multi-quarter platform builds.
- **Security posture** — read-only over segmented OT/IT; client keeps data and the models trained on it.
- **Aerospace/maritime reliability pedigree** — team built condition-monitoring & digital-twin systems at Rolls-Royce (aerospace/maritime); the heavy-asset reliability discipline data-platform vendors lack. *(Team background only — not an RR endorsement.)*
- **Sequenced trust** — core analytics now, agents later; nothing acts on its own until the core is hardened.

## Honest watch-outs (don't kid yourself)
- AVEVA, Palantir, AspenTech, Metso are larger and funded; at super-majors they may be entrenched — that's why your beachhead is single/few-site operators, not Rio Tinto-scale.
- "Live production reference" rests on Engebø; land a second reference fast.
- Several rivals own a relationship on-site already; ONE's wedge is "the layer above," not displacement.
