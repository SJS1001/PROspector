# Digitalrain — ONE for Mining (Pitch Deck Script)

*Extracted from `Digitalrain ONE for Mining.pptx` — 10 slides. The on-slide visuals are images (no extractable body text); below are the full speaker notes: the **SAY** script and the presenter **GUIDANCE** for each slide.*

---

## Slide 1 — Opening

**SAY:** Good morning, and thank you. This is ONE, from Digitalrain. In one line: ONE is the decision layer for a mine's uptime, recovery and throughput. It pulls your equipment, process, lab and maintenance data into one operating model. In the next ten minutes I'll show you the problem it solves, prove it is already running at Engebø, and tell you exactly how to start.

**GUIDANCE:** Land the one-liner, then pause. Do not mention AI or autonomy here; that comes later, and overselling it now costs credibility.

---

## Slide 2 — The Problem

**SAY:** Here is the problem. The data you need to run the plant well already exists. It is just trapped. It sits in OEM systems and vendor portals, so failures surface late and downtime runs long. Decisions run on spreadsheets and shift reports, hours behind the process, so recovery and throughput drift before anyone corrects them. And ramp-up and capacity gaps quietly cost you money every shift. This is not a sensor problem. It is an access and decision problem.

**GUIDANCE:** Read the room for the pain; let an operator nod before you move on. The three cards are headlines only, the detail is yours to deliver.

---

## Slide 3 — The Shift

**SAY:** So the shift we are making is from dashboards that describe to a decision layer that acts. ONE does five things. It ingests your sources. It harmonizes them into one model. It decides, using rules, analytics and simulation. It executes, into real tasks and alerts. And it learns. Put simply: historians store the data and dashboards show it; ONE decides on it and drives the work.

**GUIDANCE:** Those last two sentences are your differentiator against incumbents, so slow down and hit them. Gesture to the live demo as proof this is real, not a concept.

---

## Slide 4 — The Operating Model

**SAY:** Concretely, ONE is one operating model for your assets and processes, in a single view your managers and operators share. Five modules feed one decision. Asset health tells you what each asset can safely do right now. Zones tell you where a decision applies. Work shows what is already being done about it. Schedule shows what it costs in planned capacity. And intelligence tells you what ONE recommends, and why.

**GUIDANCE:** Keep this tight; it is orientation, not a feature tour. If they want depth, offer the demo rather than narrating every module.

---

## Slide 5 — The Proof (Engebø, Nordic Mining)

**SAY:** This is not a concept. At Engebø, with Nordic Mining, ONE is live on real plant data. You are looking at the digital twin and real telemetry running in the platform. The metrics on screen are the pilot's live operating model. And I will be straight with you: treat them as illustrative of what the pilot shows, not an audited result.

**GUIDANCE:** The honesty about illustrative versus audited builds trust, so do not skip it. If asked, confirm Engebø is a named, permissioned reference.

---

## Slide 6 — Architecture & Integration

**SAY:** It works with the stack you already run, and we are honest about what is live versus on the roadmap. Live today: Grafana, and XLedger inventory and maintenance. Ingested: sensors, lab, tags, OEM and ERP. On the roadmap: SCADA, historian and CMMS. And it connects read-only, over a segmented OT and IT link. Your data, and the models trained on it, stay yours.

**GUIDANCE:** For a technical or security audience, the read-only and ownership line is the unlock, so deliver it deliberately. Never present a roadmap item as live.

---

## Slide 7 — The Economics

**SAY:** Let's talk money. This is an illustrative model, a base case on rod mills, not Engebø. On one decision it shows about 1.35 million dollars a year: 700 thousand from reduced downtime, 400 thousand from quality uplift, 144 thousand from reduced maintenance, and 108 thousand from avoided repairs. It is modeled. The real number comes from a baseline on your site.

**GUIDANCE:** Say illustrative and modeled out loud; a CFO will respect the discipline. Use this to set up the diagnostic as how we get their real number.

---

## Slide 8 — The Path

**SAY:** So here is how you start. A time-boxed, roughly eight-week diagnostic on one decision. Weeks one to two, we connect two or three priority systems. Weeks three to five, we model one high-value decision. Weeks six to eight, we measure your site-specific baseline and the value at stake. On pricing: a fixed-scope diagnostic fee, and if you continue, an annual subscription plus integration, priced to your site.

**GUIDANCE:** Emphasize low risk and time-boxed; this is the ask that earns a yes. Have the fixed fee ready if procurement asks.

---

## Slide 9 — The Roadmap

**SAY:** And where it goes. Notice this is the first time I have mentioned AI, and that is deliberate. The core comes first. Today, analytics run on your live data to predict and guide. Next, agentic workflows trigger recommendations and tasks inside your processes. Later, a full 3D operating context, plant first, then the physical mine twin. Each step earns the next; we harden the core before anything acts on its own.

**GUIDANCE:** Frame this as earned trust, not autonomy, and never say autonomous mining. If they are AI-skeptical, lean on core first; if AI-curious, paint the destination.

---

## Slide 10 — The Ask

**SAY:** So that is the ask. Book the eight-week operating-model diagnostic. One decision, one baseline, a number you can trust. My contact is on screen, and Engebø is your proof this already runs. Let's find your number, on your data. Thank you.

**GUIDANCE:** End on the offer, not the features. Stop talking after the ask and let them respond; the silence does the work.
